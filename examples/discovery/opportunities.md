# Four editorial handoffs to try

English · [繁體中文](opportunities.zh-TW.md) · [Examples](../README.md)

These synthetic cases explore work outside the scored study. Each A/B pair addresses the same audience in the same medium. Give each new reader one draft and ask what they noticed first and which words led them there. For the handoff, give a different editor A, B, C and the update. The observations below are questions for a future trial.

## Citizen science · More sightings on the same route

A volunteer coordinator writes two newsletter openings for bird counters. The log records sightings, and a bird can be seen more than once.

- **A** “We logged 15 sightings on Saturday, up from 12 last Saturday. Both outings lasted four hours.”
- **B** “Four hours on the route each Saturday gave us a common basis for comparison. We logged 12 sightings on the first outing and 15 on the second.”
- **C** A tally for the data-entry volunteer reads “First outing, 12 sightings. Second outing, 15 sightings.” The hours are recorded separately.

**Update.** The timekeeper corrects the second outing to six hours. Can a reader point to the opening that first drew attention to effort? Can the editor preserve both counts and C, correct the hours in A/B, and explain why sightings per hour fell from 3 to 2.5 even though the count rose? Neither visit establishes how the local bird population changed.

## Committee secretary · Who moved it, who decided?

A secretary drafts two email openings for the same committee members. Lin proposed a Friday evening desk trial. The decision register initially shows a pending vote.

- **A** “Lin proposed opening the desk on Friday evenings. The vote on a trial is pending.”
- **B** “The trial still awaits a vote. Lin brought the Friday evening desk proposal to the committee.”
- **C** The index for the minutes archivist reads “Friday evening desk trial — motion introduced by Lin.”

**Update.** Signed minutes confirm the committee adopted a **two-Friday trial**. Ask readers what the email first told them about the speaker or the decision. In the handoff, check whether A/B state who adopted the trial and its two-Friday limit. C still identifies the mover. Turning Lin into the decision-maker or announcing permanent opening hours changes the record.

## Print production · Two floor plans named plan.pdf

A production coordinator writes two job-ticket openings for the press operator. R7 places the information desk by the east door. R8 places it by the west door. Both exports are named `plan.pdf`. The print order initially specifies R7.

- **A** “Print R7. Its plan places the information desk by the east door.”
- **B** “Look for the information desk by the east door on the proof. The order calls for R7.”
- **C** The archive index lists both revision IDs, their desk locations and their shared filename.

**Update.** The client changes the print order to R8. Which opening made the operator notice the revision or the visual detail first? Both job tickets should now point to R8 and the west door. The archive should retain both entries. Ask the operator to name the proof they would inspect before printing. The handoff records the intended revision. The filename alone cannot identify the supplied file.

## Live theatre · When should the surtitle appear?

A surtitle editor drafts two cue-card openings for the projection operator. The cue plan assigns the title “Home” to the stage manager’s GO-12 after the line “There you are.”

- **A** “On the stage manager’s GO-12, show ‘Home’. Expect the call after ‘There you are.’”
- **B** “After ‘There you are,’ wait for the stage manager’s GO-12 before showing ‘Home’.”
- **C** Yesterday’s rehearsal note for the director reads “GO-12 was called after the line. The projection screen was out of my sight.”

**Update.** The stage manager moves GO-12 to the sound of the latch. Which opening did readers use to tell the operator when to act? Check whether the editor updates both cards, keeps the stage manager’s call as the trigger and leaves yesterday’s note intact. A later rehearsal with an observer watching the screen could establish when the audience actually saw the title.
