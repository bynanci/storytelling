# Research-informed workflows

English · [繁體中文](research-workflows.zh-TW.md) · [Rules](editorial-rules.md) · [Evidence](research-foundations.md)

These six workflows are capabilities of the existing `storytelling` skill. They are not six separately installed skills and introduce no new tools. Load only the relevant sections. For a short draft, use W1 and the applicable source check; do not require a research report. All workflows inherit the main skill's bounded repair limits.

## W1 · Frame intent and sources

**Trigger:** notes or mixed material need a first draft. **Inputs:** requested audience, purpose, language, form, supplied material and constraints. If a nonessential item is missing, state a reasonable assumption; ask only for missing material or a premise when both are absent.

1. State what the audience should understand or notice, without promising a feeling.
2. List necessary sources and how they were accessed. Assign stable IDs only when reuse or revision needs them.
3. Separate supplied detail, interpretation, fiction premise and variant-only invention. Keep contradictions visible.
4. Deliver the requested draft first, with concise source notes after it.

**Output:** draft, small intent/source map and unresolved items. **Stop:** a usable draft exists, or a necessary source is missing; deliver a limited draft when possible. **Rules:** ST-R01–ST-R03, ST-R14. **Basis:** P01, P04, S01.

**Try:** a photograph description says “two people at a gate”; notes do not identify who repaired it. Keep identities unknown. Do not invent a quote or claim to have viewed the photo.

## W2 · Design narrative alternatives

**Trigger:** compare openings, a screenplay reveal, a long story or branching ideas. **Inputs:** W1 material, established fiction facts, locked facts and the author's selected treatment.

1. For long work, outline beats and relevant goals. For a brief work, use a short sequence.
2. Record occurrence order separately from reveal order; mark who knows each necessary clue.
3. Draft two alternatives that vary a named choice. Preserve unresolved branches when brainstorming.
4. Check explicit constraints. When a treatment changes, route through W5: check that an overlay or full-frame replacement still preserves required environmental context and disclosure timing. Keep aesthetic choices open; preserve an ambiguous or open ending when requested.

**Output:** A/B drafts, invariant list and choice notes. **Stop:** both drafts are usable or a constraint conflict needs a decision. **Rules:** ST-R04–ST-R06, ST-R08. **Basis:** P01–P03, P06.

**Try:** a character loses a key before entering a hall, but the audience learns about the loss later. A nonlinear telling is valid. An association A↔B is not an execution prerequisite cycle.

## W3 · Adapt across media

**Trigger:** the same material needs speech, illustration, audio, video or a localized variant. **Inputs:** selected material, audience, access needs, available media and the host's actual capabilities.

1. Identify what must carry across versions: facts, attribution, reveal, permissions and necessary context.
2. Assign a first attention cue for each medium. Use narration, silence, layout or shot choice deliberately; retain accessibility constraints.
3. Produce the requested scripts or briefs. Label proposed effects and estimated durations.
4. For a requested file, use an available authorized tool. Open/play the artifact or report that inspection is pending. Keep unsupported production work explicit.

**Output:** per-medium deliverables, source map, artifact status and missing assets. **Stop:** the requested form is inspected, or a capability/input is absent; return the usable brief. **Rules:** ST-R01, ST-R03, ST-R07, ST-R11, ST-R14. **Basis:** P03, P08, P09.

**Try:** a radio invitation and poster use the same date. Synthetic taps do not establish that a workshop was recorded. A poster-only yellow arc does not become an observed object in the radio copy.

## W4 · Audit evidence and claims

**Trigger:** factual, business, documentary or educational claims need review. **Inputs:** draft, cited source revisions, relevant passages, intended audience and limitations.

1. Split each compound assertion into independently checkable propositions.
2. Check existence, revision, relevance and support separately. Use `supported` (the available evidence supports this proposition), `contradicted` (it establishes an incompatible proposition), `insufficient` (available evidence does not establish this proposition), or `unavailable` (the necessary evidence cannot be accessed or was not supplied), with an explanation. These are review labels, not current Rust enums. No result or a completed build alone does not establish an import failure or lack of platform support. Reapply this check to revised comparisons, causal links and verification scopes from W5.
3. Distinguish exact quotation, paraphrase, inference and promotional proposal. Preserve uncertainty and scope qualifiers.
4. Offer a supported rewrite or mark a claim unresolved. Do not repair missing evidence by inventing a citation.

**Output:** claim → passage/revision → judgment → proposed wording, followed by the revised draft. **Stop:** every material claim has support or a visible unresolved disposition. **Rules:** ST-R01, ST-R02, ST-R10. **Basis:** P07, S01.

**Try:** three internal trials measured import time. They do not establish universal productivity or reliability. A source link existing is insufficient to pass the wording.

## W5 · Revise and hand off

**Trigger:** a source, permission, character fact or variant choice changes. **Inputs:** baseline drafts, source map/history, change request and protected choices.

1. Freeze baseline identities. Distinguish a shared source correction from a variant-only edit.
2. List directly affected drafts and indirect effects. Check (a) unlisted cross-draft paraphrases and relative dates, (b) derived differences, ratios and percentages against the corrected values and denominator, and (c) adjacent interpretations whose supporting detail has narrowed or disappeared. Also inspect dependencies of later reveals and composition changes: a full-frame card can obscure context previously visible behind an inset. If coverage is incomplete, review the full affected output.
3. Make the smallest complete authorized revision, including dependent wording and arithmetic. Retain unaffected content, prior versions and declined alternatives. Do not silently widen an ambiguous change. Whenever you change a comparison, causal connector or verification scope, run W4/ST-R02 on the new sentence: do not infer failure or lack of support from missing or partial evidence (`insufficient`/`unavailable`). Use `contradicted` only when evidence establishes a proposition incompatible with the claim under review. If a quote no longer mentions a button, narrow its adjacent interpretation to what the corrected quote actually supports. On consent withdrawal, remove the covered personal account and its paraphrases rather than laundering it through anonymity or another speaker.
4. Return actual revised copies and unchanged copies where requested. Show the diff, source changes, open questions and resulting artifact status.
5. If a supported CLI is available, use it only with documented inputs. Keep its direct-reference report separate from the semantic review.

**Output:** revised drafts, change/keep/review map, decisions and handoff notes. **Stop:** each affected draft has a disposition, or a decision/capability remains unresolved. **Rules:** ST-R02, ST-R03, ST-R08–ST-R11, ST-R14. **Basis:** P04, P05, P08, P11, S01.

**Try:** Saturday changes to Sunday in a poster and two radio drafts; the source drawing and synthetic taps remain reusable. Search implicit “tomorrow” references manually. Recoloring only illustration B must not recolor A.

## W6 · Evaluate audience and revision outcomes

**Trigger:** assess whether readers perceive a choice or editors make fewer errors. **Inputs:** frozen outputs, population/language/medium, independent facilitator key, comparison conditions and actual observations, if any.

1. Keep author intent and keys out of reader packets. Use neutral artifact names. Counterbalance first exposures. Ask open questions before naming a contrast. Record `rating` for first focus/reveal independently from `inference_status`: `grounded`, `hypothesis`, `unsupported`, `none` or `unassessed`. Preserve each relevant inference clause and its evidence; an unsupported assertion remains visible even when first focus matches. Missing inference fields in older records remain `unassessed`.
2. For handoffs, compare `notes` with `mapped` using matched tasks and balanced order. A separate `mapped` versus `mapped-bounds` comparison adds only the selected source ID/support/limits rows to the same map and materials, with damaged/received drafts as its predeclared primary measure. Keep conditions and comparisons separate; do not give recipients moderator answers or calibration examples. Recheck limits against the change card, which supersedes the old value. Keep setup/map/boundary-preparation time separate from handoff time. A supplied-map study does not measure the full skill's value.
3. Collect actual drafts, responses and times. Preserve incomplete attempts, disagreements and unexpected interpretations. Use another rater for disputed classifications.
4. Report A and B separately: matching first-focus readings/first exposures (`rating`), plus the distribution of `inference_status` and its denominator. Separately report `unsupported` causal assertions and the number `unassessed`; an unassessed record is not a supported reading. For handoffs, report completion/attempts, missed/expected drafts, unnecessary/received unaffected drafts, damaged/received drafts and unreturned/requested drafts by condition, case and card when possible. Use not-applicable for a zero denominator. Keep the two reading axes and distinct handoff error rates separate.
5. Follow the repository's [reading and handoff protocol](https://github.com/bynanci/storytelling/blob/main/examples/evaluation/README.md). Use the [source limits](https://github.com/bynanci/storytelling/blob/main/examples/evaluation/source-boundaries.md) only in the assigned handoff arm; keep the [account-review calibration](https://github.com/bynanci/storytelling/blob/main/examples/studies/account-review/calibration.md) with facilitators. Its sample thresholds are local pilot decisions, not paper-derived effect sizes. Freeze thresholds before recruitment; decisions must account for missed, unnecessary, damaged and unreturned drafts, rather than speed or omissions alone. Keep N-01 and this evaluation separate.
6. Use model critique only to propose failure cases. Label agent rehearsals as synthetic. If there are no human observations, deliver a protocol and blank result fields.

**Output:** protocol or evidence-backed outcome report with continue/narrow/revise/stop recommendation and limitations. **Stop:** the predeclared trial is complete or observations are unavailable; never manufacture a successful participant. **Rules:** ST-R06, ST-R12, ST-R13. **Basis:** P03, P05, P10.

**Try:** two variants both lead readers to the date. Preserve that result and revise the cue. A machine order check passing does not overturn the observation.

## Contribution and extraction criteria

Keep one router while these workflows share source and revision concepts. Split a workflow into a separate installable skill only after it has a distinct discovery trigger, self-contained references, an independent synthetic evaluation and an identified maintainer. Test the complete copied package, not only repository-relative links. Preserve the skill license and cite research through original summaries. Do not require a remote model, paid service or Rust installation for manual use.
